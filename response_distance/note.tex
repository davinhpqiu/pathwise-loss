\large Response distance and tensor certificates}

\begin{abstract}
How different do two driving paths look to linear systems with bounded matrix
coefficients? This discussion draft measures their separation by worst-case
terminal-response difference and compares it with a tensor certificate: a
weighted Hilbert--Schmidt signature difference that bounds response distance
from above. Two structured comparisons quantify the gap. For planar
bounded-variation paths and their degree-two log-ODE approximations, a fixed
partition with nonzero cubic discrepancy gives small-amplitude ratio
\(2\sqrt2/9\), uniformly in response dimension at least two. For chains of at
least three adjacent area-only blocks, explicit separation persists at every
positive amplitude, with bounds independent of chain length.
\end{abstract}

\section{Response distance and tensor geometry}
We ask how bounded linear systems distinguish paths, and how response distance
compares with tensor-norm differences between their signatures or approximations.

\paragraph{Response distance.}
Let \(X,\widetilde X:[0,1]\to\R^d\) be continuous paths of finite Euclidean
variation, defined for \(X\) by
\[
 \norm{X}_{1\text{-var}}
 =\sup_\tau\sum_{[u,v]\in\tau}\norm{X_v-X_u}_2,
\]
where \(\tau\) ranges over finite partitions into intervals and
\(\norm{\cdot}_2\) is Euclidean norm. BV abbreviates bounded variation.
For a tuple \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_d)\) of real \(n\times n\) matrices, let
\(\Phi^A(X)=Y_1\) be terminal response of
\[
 dY=\sum_i YA_i\,dX^i,\qquad Y_0=I_n,
\]
where \(I_n\) is identity. Input dimension is \(d\); response dimension is
\(n\). Fix joint coefficient budget
\(|A|^2=\sum_i\norm{A_i}_F^2\le r^2\), \(r>0\), using Frobenius norm
\(\norm{A_i}_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(A_i^\top A_i)\).
Use Euclidean operator norm
\(\norm{M}_{\op}=\sup_{\norm{v}_2=1}\norm{Mv}_2\). Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \Del_{r,n}(X,\widetilde X)
 =\sup_{|A|\le r}\norm{\Phi^A(X)-\Phi^A(\widetilde X)}_{\op}.
\end{equation}
This measures largest terminal-response difference over admissible systems
and unit initial states. It is a pseudodistance: distinct paths can have
identical responses and hence zero separation. Requiring separation at most
\(\varepsilon>0\) expresses response accuracy in
\((\varepsilon,r)\)-representation framework \cite{ferrucci2026concise}.

\paragraph{Tensor certificate.}
The signature records ordered iterated integrals:
\[
 S(X)_0=1,\qquad S(X)_j=\int_{0<u_1<\cdots<u_j<1}
 dX_{u_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes dX_{u_j},\quad j\ge1.
\]
For standard basis \((e_i)\), tensor word
\(e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_j}\) records a sequence of \(j\)
directions. Define
\(R_A(e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_j})=A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_j}\),
extended linearly over convergent series, with empty word mapped to \(I_n\).
Iterated integration gives \(\Phi^A(X)=R_A(S(X))\).

For convergent tensor series \(F,G\) with \(F_0=G_0=1\), extend
\(\Del_{r,n}\) by replacing the responses in \eqref{eq:criterion} with
\(R_A(F),R_A(G)\). Write \(F_j,G_j\) for degree components. Declare standard
word tensors orthonormal; their Hilbert--Schmidt (HS) norm is Euclidean norm
of word coefficients. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 \B_r(F,G)=\sum_{j\ge1}r^j\norm{F_j-G_j}_{\HS}.
\end{equation}
We consider pairs for which this sum is finite. Degreewise Cauchy--Schwarz
gives, for \(|A|\le r\),
\[
 \norm{R_A(F_j-G_j)}_{\op}\le r^j\norm{F_j-G_j}_{\HS},
 \qquad\Del_{r,n}(F,G)\le\B_r(F,G).
\]
Thus \(\B_r\le\varepsilon\) certifies response distance at most
\(\varepsilon\). When \(\B_r>\varepsilon\), this test leaves response
accuracy undecided. We quantify how much this tensor measure can overstate
response separation.

\paragraph{Comparison pairs.}
Degree-two approximations provide pairs with an explicit first discrepancy.
Tensor multiplication concatenates words. For \(U_0=0\), define
\(e_\otimes^U=\exp_\otimes U=\sum_{q\ge0}U^{\otimes q}/q!\), with
\(U^{\otimes0}=1\); \(\log_\otimes\) is its formal inverse on series with
constant term one. Brackets are commutators:
\([U,V]=U\otimes V-V\otimes U\) for tensors and \([A,B]=AB-BA\) for matrices.

For partition \(\pi=(0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=1)\), let
\(X_i=X|_{[t_{i-1},t_i]}\) and
\(\ell_{i,j}=(\log_\otimes S(X_i))_j\). Store
\(\ell_i^{\le2}=\ell_{i,1}+\ell_{i,2}\): displacement and signed area in
the plane. Exact signature and decoded series are
\[
 F=S(X),\qquad G_\pi=\prod_{i=1}^m\exp_\otimes(\ell_i^{\le2}).
\]
Indices \(i,j\) denote interval and tensor degree; products follow time order.
Local signatures use corresponding integration intervals.

Degree-two log-ODE decoding evaluates these retained terms in the system:
\[
 \Phi^A(X)=R_A(F),\qquad
 \widehat\Phi^A_\pi(X)=R_A(G_\pi)
 =\prod_i\exp\bigl(R_A(\ell_i^{\le2})\bigr).
\]
One tuple \(A\) acts on every interval:
\[
 X\xrightarrow{\text{split }\pi}
 \underbrace{(\ell_i^{\le2})_{i=1}^m}_{\text{stored data}}
 \xrightarrow{\text{exponentiate and compose}}\widehat\Phi^A_\pi(X).
\]

\section{A planar distance-to-certificate ratio}
First compare a path with its degree-two representation. Their response
distance measures which discarded information bounded systems can detect.
Scaling path amplitude isolates its first nonzero degree.

Fix \(d=2\) and partition \(\pi\). Interval factors agree through degree two,
so their first possible discrepancies add:
\[
 D_3=(F-G_\pi)_3=\sum_i\ell_{i,3}.
\]
Define dilation \((\delta_sF)_j=s^jF_j\). Scaling \(X\) by \(s>0\) at
coefficient budget \(r\) is equivalent to dilation by \(t=sr\) at unit budget.
Write
\[
 \Del_n(t)=\Del_{1,n}(\delta_tF,\delta_tG_\pi),\qquad
 \B(t)=\B_1(\delta_tF,\delta_tG_\pi).
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bv}
If \(D_3\ne0\), then
\[
 \frac{\Del_n(t)}{\B(t)}\longrightarrow
 \rho:=\frac{2\sqrt2}{9}\quad(t\downarrow0),
\]
uniformly in response dimension \(n\ge2\).
\end{proposition}

HS norm counts word coefficients independently. Matrix products share one
budget-constrained tuple, and cubic Lie terms become nested commutators.
A driver rotation reduces any planar cubic Lie direction to a multiple of
\([e_1,[e_1,e_2]]\). Its coefficients \(1,-2,1\) give HS norm \(\sqrt6\).
B\"ottcher--Wenzel \cite{bottcher2008frobenius} gives sharp response maximum
\(4/(3\sqrt3)\), attained in dimension two. Their quotient is \(\rho\),
so leading certificate error exceeds response error by factor
\(1/\rho\approx3.18\). Appendix~\ref{app:planar} proves the gauge and tail bounds.

\paragraph{A finite-scale separation.}
For corner \(0\to e_1\to e_1+e_2\), store displacement and area in one
interval. At unit budget,
\[
 \Phi^A(tX)=e^{tA_1}e^{tA_2},\qquad
 \widehat\Phi^A_\pi(tX)=e^{t(A_1+A_2)+(t^2/2)[A_1,A_2]}.
\]
\begin{samepage}
At \(t=1/20\), rational enclosures prove, for every \(n\ge2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner}
 \Del_n(t)<1.934\times10^{-5}<2\times10^{-5}<3.608\times10^{-5}<\B(t).
\end{equation}
These representations are within response distance \(2\times10^{-5}\),
while their tensor certificate exceeds that tolerance.
Appendix~\ref{app:planar} gives exact arithmetic.
\par
\end{samepage}

A two-interval square loop can have \(D_3=0\) and nonzero quartic error;
partition refinement can make \(D_3\) vanish. Mesh-uniform bounds and extension
to rough inputs with roughness exponent \(2<p<3\) remain open.

\section{Area-chain distance separation at every amplitude}
Our second comparison replaces an ordered product of area blocks by an
exponential of their sum. Total area is preserved; order can affect
higher-degree tensors and matrix responses.
For \(\ell\ge3\), \(d=\ell+1\), set \(U_i=[e_i,e_{i+1}]\),
\(1\le i\le\ell\), and compare
\[
 F_\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^\ell e_\otimes^{\lambda U_i},\qquad
 G_\lambda=e_\otimes^{\lambda\sum_iU_i},\qquad\lambda>0.
\]
Here \(\lambda\) is area amplitude. Write
\(\Del_n(\lambda)=\Del_{1,n}(F_\lambda,G_\lambda)\) and
\(\B(\lambda)=\B_1(F_\lambda,G_\lambda)\).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chain}
For every \(\ell\ge3\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\lambda>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
 \Del_n(\lambda)
 <\frac{\lambda^2}{\sqrt2}e^{\sqrt2\lambda}
 <\frac{e^{\sqrt6\lambda}-1-\sqrt6\lambda}{\sqrt3}
 \le\B(\lambda).
\end{equation}
Bounds are independent of chain length.
\end{proposition}

This separates the response and tensor measures for the stated pair at every
amplitude. If \(F_\lambda\) itself approximates a BV path, add that initial
response error by triangle inequality. Appendix~\ref{app:chain} proves the bounds.
Compression is one consequence: under fixed generic depth-two layout,
replacing \(\ell\) records by one reduces storage by factor \(\ell\) at
tolerances inside the proved gap.

\paragraph{Prior work and scope.}
The \((\varepsilon,r)\)-representation paper supplies response criterion and
identifies first discarded local log-signature error after (2.10)
\cite{ferrucci2026concise}. Proofs use B\"ottcher--Wenzel
\cite{bottcher2008frobenius} and a two-factor commutator error bound
\cite{iserles2024elementary}. Independent proof review and novelty
confirmation remain pending.

\section{Planar gauge, tails and finite example}\label{app:planar}
Let \(Z=[e_1,e_2]\). Every planar cubic Lie element is \(D=[w,Z]\),
\(w\in\R^2\), with \(\norm{D}_{\HS}=\sqrt6\norm{w}_2\).
Proper driver rotations preserve tuple ball and fix \(Z\), reducing to
\(D=\norm{w}_2[e_1,Z]\). B\"ottcher--Wenzel applied twice gives
\[
 \norm{[A_1,[A_1,A_2]]}_{\op}
 \le2\norm{A_1}_F^2\norm{A_2}_F\le\frac4{3\sqrt3}\quad(|A|\le1).
\]
Equality holds at
\[
 A_1=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_2=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Embedding in dimension \(n\ge2\) therefore proves
\(\sup_{|A|\le1}\norm{R_A(D)}_{\op}=\rho\norm{D}_{\HS}\).
One commutator step also gives
\(\sup_{|A|\le1}\norm{R_A(\alpha Z)}_{\op}
 \le|\alpha|/\sqrt2=\norm{\alpha Z}_{\HS}/2\), \(\alpha\in\R\).

\paragraph{Full-series tails.}
Put \(L=\norm{X}_{1\text{-var}}\), \(h=\norm{D_3}_{\HS}\),
\(a=\sum_i\norm{\ell_{i,1}}_2\), and
\(b=\sum_i\norm{\ell_{i,2}}_{\HS}\). Define
\[
 T_4(a,b;t)=e^{at+bt^2}-1-at-(a^2/2+b)t^2-(a^3/6+ab)t^3,
\]
\(Q_R(t)=T_4(L,0;t)+T_4(a,b/2;t)\), and
\(Q_H(t)=T_4(L,0;t)+T_4(a,b;t)\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
 |\Del_n(t)-\rho ht^3|\le Q_R(t),\qquad
 ht^3\le\B(t)\le ht^3+Q_H(t).
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\norm{S(X)_j}_{\HS}\le L^j/j!\) gives majorant \(e^{Lt}\).
Tensor products give decoder majorant \(e^{at+bt^2}\); area response bound
improves this to \(e^{at+(b/2)t^2}\). Removing degrees zero through three
gives \(Q_R,Q_H\). Cubic discrepancy is \(\sum_i\ell_{i,3}\), since
multiplication by nonconstant factors raises degree. Cubic gauge and triangle
inequality give response bound in \eqref{eq:tails}; degree-three certificate
term gives its lower bound. Hence
\[
 \left|\frac{\Del_n(t)}{\B(t)}-\rho\right|
 \le\frac{Q_R(t)+\rho Q_H(t)}{ht^3}=O(t).
\]
Tail constants are independent of \(n\), proving Proposition~\ref{prop:bv}.
In particular, \(Q_R(t)<(1-\rho)ht^3\) implies \(\Del_n(t)<\B(t)\).

\paragraph{Rational corner certificate.}
For the one-interval corner,
\[
 D_3=([e_1,Z]-[e_2,Z])/12,\quad
 L=2,\quad h=\sqrt3/6,\quad a=\sqrt2,\quad b=\sqrt2/2.
\]
For rational \(x\ge0\), integer \(J\ge0\), and \(x<J+2\), put
\(P_J(x)=\sum_{j=0}^Jx^j/j!\). Successive omitted-term ratios give
\[
 P_J(x)\le e^x\le P_J(x)
 +\frac{x^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}\frac1{1-x/(J+2)}.
\]
Use \(J=24\) and rational bounds, verified by integer squares,
\[
 \sqrt2<707107/500000,\quad
 \sqrt3>34641/20000,\quad\sqrt6<244949/100000.
\]
At \(t=1/20\), apply these to \(\rho ht^3+Q_R(t)\), using
\(\rho h=\sqrt6/27\) and nonnegative coefficients of \(T_4\).
The lower bound \(ht^3\) for \(\B(t)\) then proves \eqref{eq:corner}
with exact fractions.

\section{All-scale chain proof}\label{app:chain}
For \(|A|\le1\), put \(C_i=[A_i,A_{i+1}]\),
\(a_i=\norm{A_i}_F\), and \(c_\ell=\cos(\pi/(\ell+2))<1\).
The path-graph largest eigenvalue gives
\(\sum_{i=1}^\ell a_i a_{i+1}\le c_\ell\sum_i a_i^2\le c_\ell\).
B\"ottcher--Wenzel gives
\[
 M:=\sup_{|A|\le1}\sum_i\norm{C_i}_{\op}\le\sqrt2c_\ell,
 \qquad
 K:=\sup_{|A|\le1}\sum_{i<j}\norm{[C_i,C_j]}_{\op}
 \le\sqrt2c_\ell^2.
\]
For the second bound, use
\(\norm{[C_i,C_j]}_{\op}\le2\sqrt2
 (a_i a_{i+1})(a_j a_{j+1})\), then sum pairs.
For real square matrices \(P,Q\), two-factor commutator bound
\cite{iserles2024elementary} is
\[
 \norm{e^{\lambda P}e^{\lambda Q}-e^{\lambda(P+Q)}}_{\op}
 \le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\norm{[P,Q]}_{\op}
 e^{\lambda(\norm{P}_{\op}+\norm{Q}_{\op})}.
\]
Differentiate \(e^{(\lambda-s)(P+Q)}e^{sP}e^{sQ}\) and use
\([e^{sP},Q]=\int_0^s e^{(s-u)P}[P,Q]e^{uP}\,du\).
Induction over factors gives
\[
 \Del_n(\lambda)\le\frac{\lambda^2K}{2}e^{\lambda M}
 <\frac{\lambda^2}{\sqrt2}e^{\sqrt2\lambda}.
\]

Write \(F_\lambda-G_\lambda=\sum_{m\ge2}\lambda^mD_{2m}\) and
\(H_m=m!\norm{D_{2m}}_{\HS}\); tensor degree is \(2m\).
Blocks \(U_i\) are orthogonal with norm \(\sqrt2\). Among words of length
\(m\) over \(U_1,U_2,U_3\), exactly \(\binom{m+2}{2}\) are nondecreasing.
Other words are absent from ordered product, and have coefficient \(1/m!\)
in merged exponential. Orthogonality gives
\[
 H_m^2\ge2^m\left(3^m-\binom{m+2}{2}\right)\ge2^m3^{m-1},\quad m\ge2.
\]
The last inequality holds at \(m=2\) and follows by induction from
\(\binom{m+3}{2}/\binom{m+2}{2}=(m+3)/(m+1)<3\).
Summing \(\B(\lambda)=\sum_{m\ge2}\lambda^mH_m/m!\) proves last bound
in \eqref{eq:chain}.

For middle inequality, ratio of degree-\(m\) scalar coefficients is
\(L_m/\sqrt2\), where
\[
 L_m=\frac{4\,3^{(m-1)/2}}{m(m-1)},\qquad
 \frac{L_{m+1}}{L_m}=\sqrt3\,\frac{m-1}{m+1}.
\]
This ratio is below one for \(m\le3\) and above one for \(m\ge4\).
Thus \(\inf_{m\ge2}L_m=L_4=\sqrt3>\sqrt2\), proving strict coefficient
comparison at every degree and Proposition~\ref{prop:chain}.
