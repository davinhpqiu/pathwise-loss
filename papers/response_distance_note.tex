\begin{abstract}
A Hilbert--Schmidt signature certificate can reject a representation whose
worst-case linear-system response error is acceptable. For ordinary planar
bounded-variation paths, degree-two log-ODE distortion has sharp small-scale
response/certificate ratio \(2\sqrt2/9\), with explicit finite-scale and
perturbation bounds. Homogeneous area chains give a separation at every
positive scale. An exact bounded-variation Euler minimax constraint changes
finite integer storage optima while preserving their leading asymptotics.
Proofs and reproducible examples are included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Criterion and comparison}
The response criterion of Ferrucci, Perr\'ee and Lyons (FPL)
\cite{ferrucci2026concise} motivates retaining a path's effect on bounded
linear systems while reducing storage. Fix \(d\) driver channels and \(n\)
response states. Real matrix tuples \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_d)\) have joint budget
\[
 |A|^2=\sum_i\norm{A_i}_F^2\le r^2.
\]
Tensor words are evaluated chronologically:
\(R_A(e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_j})=A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_j}\).
This is the row-state convention \(dY=\sum_iYA_i\,dX^i\); transpose tuples
to recover column states. Starting at identity,
\(\Phi^A(X)=R_A(S(X))\) is the terminal response operator.
For full tensor series \(F,G\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \Del_{r,n}(F,G)=\sup_{|A|\le r}\norm{R_A(F)-R_A(G)}_{\op},\qquad
 \B_r(F,G)=\sum_{j\ge1}r^j\norm{F_j-G_j}_{\HS}.
\end{equation}
Here operator norm is Euclidean, and standard word tensors are orthonormal
for HS norm. In the classes below these series converge absolutely and
\(\Del_{r,n}\le\B_r\).
Full HS certificate, a further length-based envelope, and true response error
are different constraints. Straight scalar developments can attain their
HS certificate; a restricted Lie-direction gap is compatible with that fact.

\section{Planar bounded-variation distortion}
Let \(X:[0,1]\to\R^2\) be continuous with finite Euclidean variation \(L\).
Fix a partition \(\pi\); \(X_i\) is the restriction to interval \(i\).
Write \(\ell_{i,j}=(\log_\otimes S(X_i))_j\) for its degree-\(j\)
local log-signature, and define
\[
 F=S(X),\qquad G_\pi=\prod_i\exp_\otimes(\ell_{i,1}+\ell_{i,2}),\qquad
 D_3=\sum_i\ell_{i,3},\quad h=\norm{D_3}_{\HS}.
\]
Dilation \(\delta_s\) multiplies tensor degree \(j\) by \(s^j\).
Coefficient radius \(r\) and path scale \(s\) combine into \(t=sr\).
Set \(\Del_n(t)=\Del_{1,n}(\delta_t F,\delta_t G_\pi)\) and
\(\B(t)=\B_1(\delta_t F,\delta_t G_\pi)\).
Homogeneity gives \(\Del_{r,n}(\delta_sF,\delta_sG_\pi)=\Del_n(sr)\).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bv}
If \(h>0\), then for every \(n\ge2\),
\[
 \frac{\Del_n(t)}{\B(t)}\longrightarrow
 \rho:=\frac{2\sqrt2}{9}\quad(t\downarrow0),
\]
uniformly in \(n\). In particular, the asymptotic HS/response
overestimation factor is \(1/\rho=9/(2\sqrt2)\), approximately \(3.18\).
Explicit tails below give a positive finite-scale separation radius and
uniformity on a BV perturbation neighborhood.
\end{theorem}

Put \(a=\sum_i\norm{\ell_{i,1}}_2\), \(b=\sum_i\norm{\ell_{i,2}}_{\HS}\) and
\[
 T_4(a,b;t)=e^{at+bt^2}-1-at-(a^2/2+b)t^2-(a^3/6+ab)t^3.
\]
All coefficients in this degree-four-and-above tail are nonnegative.
Define \(Q_R=T_4(L,0;t)+T_4(a,b/2;t)\) and
\(Q_H=T_4(L,0;t)+T_4(a,b;t)\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
 |\Del_n(t)-\rho ht^3|\le Q_R(t),\qquad
 ht^3\le\B(t)\le ht^3+Q_H(t).
\end{equation}
For any reference \(T>0\), let \(C_T=Q_R(T)/T^4\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius}
 t_*=\min\{T,(1-\rho)h/(2C_T)\},\qquad c=(1+\rho)/2.
\end{equation}
For \(0<t\le t_*\), \(\Del_n(t)\le cht^3<ht^3\le\B(t)\).
Appendix~\ref{app:planar} proves these assertions and perturbation stability.

\paragraph{A finite decision with exact arithmetic.}
For \(0\to e_1\to e_1+e_2\) on one interval,
\[
 D_3=([e_1,Z]-[e_2,Z])/12,\quad Z=[e_1,e_2],\quad
 h=\sqrt3/6,\quad a=\sqrt2,\quad b=\sqrt2/2.
\]
At \(t=1/20\), rational Taylor enclosures prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner}
 \Del_n(t)<1.934\,10^{-5}<2\,10^{-5}<3.608\,10^{-5}<\B(t).
\end{equation}
Thus tolerance \(2\,10^{-5}\) accepts this approximation under a proved
response bound and rejects its full HS certificate. Appendix~\ref{app:cert}
gives the enclosure, without optimizer or floating-point rounding.

The partition and \(h>0\) are essential. A two-interval square loop has
zero cubic discrepancy but nonzero quartic error. Mesh refinement can make
\(h\) vanish. Theorem~\ref{thm:bv} supplies no mesh-uniform conclusion or
extension to general rough input with \(2<p<3\).

\section{Homogeneous merges at every positive scale}
For \(\ell\ge3\), in driver dimension \(d=\ell+1\), let
\(U_i=[e_i,e_{i+1}]\) and compare decoded depth-two series
\[
 F_\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}e_\otimes^{\lambda U_i},\qquad
 G_\lambda=e_\otimes^{\lambda\sum_iU_i},\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
Use unit tuple budget in \(\Del_n(\lambda)=\Del_{1,n}(F_\lambda,G_\lambda)\)
and \(\B(\lambda)=\B_1(F_\lambda,G_\lambda)\).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chain}
For every \(\ell\ge3\), every response dimension \(n\ge1\) and \(\lambda>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
 \Del_n(\lambda)
 <\frac{\lambda^2}{\sqrt2}e^{\sqrt2\lambda}
 <\frac{e^{\sqrt6\lambda}-1-\sqrt6\lambda}{\sqrt3}
 \le\B(\lambda).
\end{equation}
The separation bounds are independent of chain length.
\end{theorem}

This permits an \(\ell\)-to-one merge at a nonempty interval of response
tolerances rejected by the full certificate. With generic depth-two
log-signature layout held fixed, storage falls by factor \(\ell\).
The result concerns the displayed decoded factors. For ordinary coordinate
loops, add the fine decoder's original-path error to assess total error.
Equivalently, the fine series is exact for a concatenation of pure area
rough blocks. Appendix~\ref{app:chain} verifies the infinitely many coefficient
comparisons analytically; a finite coefficient scan would be insufficient.

\section{Exact Euler minimax and storage}
For degree-\(N\) Euler decoding on \(m\) intervals, put
\(P_N(x)=\sum_{j=0}^Nx^j/j!\), and
\[
 E^A_{N,\pi}(X)=\prod_i
 \left[I+\sum_{j=1}^NR_{A,j}(S(X_i)_j)\right].
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:euler}
For any \(N,m\ge1\), fixed driver and response dimensions, coefficient radius
\(r>0\), and paths of variation at most one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
 \sup_X\ \inf_{\pi:\,m\ {\rm intervals}}\ \sup_{|A|\le r}
 \norm{\Phi^A(X)-E^A_{N,\pi}(X)}_{\op}
 =\E(N,m,r):=e^r-P_N(r/m)^m.
\end{equation}
Partitions may depend on \(X\), but not on \(A\).
Equal-variation partitions give the upper bound; a straight line with
a scalar/rank-one development attains the minimax error.
\end{theorem}

FPL already display the scalar expression in their equation (1.13).
Appendix~\ref{app:euler} proves its universal majorant and partition
minimization. This refines the additional length envelope
\[
 B(N,m,r)=\frac{e^r r^{N+1}}{(N+1)!m^N}.
\]
Both comparisons use the same dense generic storage \(m\Lambda_d(N)\), where
\[
 \Lambda_d(N)=\sum_{j=1}^N\frac1j\sum_{v\mid j}\mu(v)d^{j/v};
\]
\(\mu\) is the M\"obius function. The decoder is Euler even though stored
coordinates are log-signature coordinates.

\paragraph{Leading asymptotics persist.}
Let \(C_{d,k}(z,\varepsilon)\) minimize \(m\Lambda_d(kq)\) under
\(\E(q,m,z)\le\varepsilon\), with fixed \(d\ge2,k\ge1\).
Put \(\beta=k\log d\), \(\lambda=z+\log(z/\varepsilon)\).
In either limit \(z\to\infty\) at fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), or
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) at fixed \(z>0\), any optimizer satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymp}
 q_*\sim\sqrt{\lambda/\beta},\qquad
 \log(m_*/z)\sim\sqrt{\beta\lambda},\qquad
 \log(C_{d,k}/z)\sim2\sqrt{\beta\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Replacing \(\E\) by \(B\) changes minimum cost by a relative factor tending
to one (Appendix~\ref{app:asymp}). For \(k=1\), this preserves the leading BV
storage asymptotics in FPL.

Pure Engel paths supply a separate restricted comparison, with response
dimension \(n\ge2\). For \(k\ge2\) and
\(E_k=\ad_{e_1}^{k-1}(e_2)\), set
\[
 h_k=\sqrt{\binom{2k-2}{k-1}},\quad
 g_k=[2(k-1)]^{(k-1)/2}k^{-k/2},\quad s_k=g_k/\sqrt2.
\]
For a pure degree-\(k\) rough path, take \(p\ge k\); effective amplitude
\(a=br^k>0\) absorbs original amplitude \(b\) and coefficient radius \(r\).
When \(\varepsilon<e^{a s_k}-1\), true Euler storage is bracketed by
\[
 C_{d,k}(a s_k,\varepsilon)\le C_k^{\rm true}(a,\varepsilon)
 \le C_{d,k}(a g_k,\varepsilon)\le C_{d,k}(a h_k,\varepsilon).
\]
Consequently, at fixed \(d,k,\varepsilon\),
\[
 \liminf_{a\to\infty}\frac1{\sqrt a}
 \log\frac{C_{d,k}(a h_k,\varepsilon)}{C_k^{\rm true}(a,\varepsilon)}
 \ge2\sqrt{k\log d}\,(\sqrt{h_k}-\sqrt{g_k})>0.
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:engel} gives the gauges, witness and scope.
A single depth-\(k\) log-ODE factor represents this pure path exactly;
the growing overhead concerns fixed Euler decoding and dense generic storage.

\section{Prior work and boundaries}
Development separation through polynomial identities is already explicit in
Chevyrev--Lyons, \S4.2 and Theorem 4.8 \cite{chevyrev2016characteristic}.
The planar degree-six matrix identity is classical \cite{nikolaev1986two};
low-degree dimension-two attainment implies no universal dimension sufficiency.
Spectral Lie-polynomial evaluations and low-degree calculations have direct
precedent in Boedihardjo--Geng--Souris \cite{boedihardjo2020developments},
with different norm budgets; exact pure-path tail asymptotics appear in
Li--Gao \cite{li2026exact}. The Engel gauge below is a
B\"ottcher--Wenzel corollary \cite{bottcher2008frobenius}.
The product-formula representation is standard
\cite{iserles2024elementary}, as is fixed-system adaptive log-ODE analysis
\cite{bayer2023adaptive}. FPL's discussion after (2.10) already identifies
the first discarded local log-signature error.

The candidate addition is the constrained response/HS comparison and its
finite-scale consequences. General rough inputs with \(2<p<3\), mesh-uniform
nondegeneracy, exact finite-scale response suprema and general cubic-zero
behavior remain open. The displayed results give no correction to FPL's
general rough-path asymptotics or its empirical averaged-error figures.

\section{Planar gauge, tails and stability}\label{app:planar}
Signature degree \(j\) is
\(S(X)_j=\int_{0<u_1<\cdots<u_j<1}dX_{u_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes dX_{u_j}\).
For homogeneous \(D\) of degree \(j\), write
\(\gamma_{j,n}(D)=\sup_{|A|\le1}\norm{R_A(D)}_{\op}\).
Let \(Z=[e_1,e_2]\). Every planar cubic Lie element is
\(D=[w,Z]\) with \(w\in\R^2\), and
\(\norm{D}_{\HS}=\sqrt6\norm{w}_2\).
Proper driver rotations preserve the tuple ball and fix \(Z\), so reduce to
\(D=\norm{w}_2[e_1,Z]\).
B\"ottcher--Wenzel and scalar maximization give
\[
 \norm{[A_1,[A_1,A_2]]}_{\op}
 \le2\norm{A_1}_F^2\norm{A_2}_F\le\frac4{3\sqrt3}.
\]
Equality holds at
\[
 A_1=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A_2=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose cubic response is \(4E_{12}/(3\sqrt3)\).
Embedding proves equality for every \(n\ge2\); hence
\(\gamma_{3,n}(D)=\rho\norm D_{\HS}\).
One commutator step similarly gives
\(\gamma_{2,n}(\alpha Z)=|\alpha|/\sqrt2=\norm{\alpha Z}_{\HS}/2\).
It is attained at \(A_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)/2\) and
\(A_2=E_{12}/\sqrt2\).

\paragraph{First discrepancy.}
Group-like series \(F,G\) agreeing through degree two have
\(D_3=F_3-G_3=(\log F)_3-(\log G)_3\), a cubic Lie tensor.
The cubic formula and uniform response tail yield
\[
 \rho ht^3-Q_R(t)\le\Del_n(t)\le\rho ht^3+Q_R(t).
\]
The certificate includes its degree-three term exactly, giving
\eqref{eq:tails}. Thus
\[
 \left|\frac{\Del_n(t)}{\B(t)}-\rho\right|
 \le\frac{Q_R(t)+\rho Q_H(t)}{ht^3}.
\]
Both tails are \(O(t^4)\), independently of \(n\).
For partitioned degree-two log-ODE, interval factors agree through degree two
and first differ by \(\ell_{i,3}\). Multiplication by any nonconstant
factor raises degree, so \(D_3=\sum_i\ell_{i,3}\).

\paragraph{Full series control.}
BV factorial bounds majorize \(F\) by \(e^{Lt}\).
Tensor products majorize \(G_\pi\) by \(e^{at+bt^2}\).
The sharp area gauge majorizes the matrix decoder by
\(e^{at+(b/2)t^2}\). Removing degrees zero to three yields \(Q_R,Q_H\).
For numerical evaluation, the positive identity
\[
 T_4(a,b;t)=e^p-P_3(p)+q(e^p-P_1(p))+e^p(e^q-P_1(q)),
 \quad p=at,\ q=bt^2,
\]
avoids small-scale subtractive cancellation.
Nonnegative coefficients imply \(Q_R(t)\le C_Tt^4\) on \(0\le t\le T\),
which proves \eqref{eq:radius} and the strict gap.

\paragraph{BV stability.}
For anchored paths of length at most \(L\) and
\(\eta=\norm{X-Y}_{1\text{-var}}\), interpolation and insertion of
one \(d(Y-X)\) into the iterated integral give
\[
 \norm{S_j(X)-S_j(Y)}_{\HS}
 \le\frac{\eta L^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}.
\]
At the insertion time, summing over positions produces
\((L_{\rm before}+L_{\rm after})^{j-1}/(j-1)!\); integrate against the
variation measure of \(Y-X\), then along interpolation.
In
\[
 \ell_3=S_3-\tfrac12(S_1\otimes S_2+S_2\otimes S_1)
              +\tfrac13 S_1^{\otimes3},
\]
the three differences have bounds \(\eta L^2/2\), \(3\eta L^2/2\),
and \(\eta L^2\). Therefore
\(\norm{\ell_3(X)-\ell_3(Y)}_{\HS}\le3\eta L^2\).
On a fixed common partition, local difference variations sum to \(\eta\);
the same bound applies to \(D_3\).
Also \(a\le L\), \(b\le\sum_iL_i^2\le L^2\).
For a baseline of length \(L_X\) and cubic norm \(h_X>0\), set
\[
 L=L_X+1,\qquad \eta_*=\min\{1,h_X/(6L^2)\}.
\]
Perturbations within \(\eta_*\) have cubic norm at least \(h_X/2\).
Use \(Q_R^*(t)=T_4(L,0;t)+T_4(L,L^2/2;t)\) and this lower norm in
\eqref{eq:radius} for a common positive radius.

\paragraph{Mixed blocks.}
For increments \(v_i\) and areas \(\alpha_iZ\), compare
\[
 e_\otimes^{tv_1+t^2\alpha_1Z}e_\otimes^{tv_2+t^2\alpha_2Z}
 \quad\hbox{with}\quad
 e_\otimes^{t(v_1+v_2)+t^2(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\omega/2)Z},
 \qquad\omega=\det(v_1,v_2).
\]
BCH at degree three gives \([w,Z]\), where
\[
 w=\tfrac12(\alpha_2v_1-\alpha_1v_2)
                  +\tfrac{\omega}{12}(v_1-v_2).
\]
The same lemma applies when \(w\ne0\), with response tails
\(T_4(a_f,b_f/2;t)+T_4(a_c,b_c/2;t)\), where
\[
 a_f=\norm{v_1}+\norm{v_2},\quad b_f=\sqrt2(|\alpha_1|+|\alpha_2|),
 \quad a_c=\norm{v_1+v_2},\quad b_c=\sqrt2|\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\omega/2|.
\]
For independent \(v_1,v_2\), \(w=0\) exactly when
\(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=-\omega/6\). Pure planar area blocks commute.

\section{Gram certificates and all-scale chain proof}\label{app:chain}
Let same-layer blocks \(U_1,\ldots,U_\ell\) have Gram matrix
\(g_{ij}=\langle U_i,U_j\rangle_{\HS}\).
For a weak composition \(\alpha\) of \(m\), let \(w(\alpha)\) be its
nondecreasing word, \(\alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!\), and
\(W_\alpha=U_1^{\otimes\alpha_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_\ell^{\otimes\alpha_\ell}\).
The meta-degree terms are
\[
 F_m=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}W_\alpha/\alpha!,
 \qquad G_m=(\sum_iU_i)^{\otimes m}/m!.
\]
With \(H_m=m!\norm{F_m-G_m}_{\HS}\), tensor inner products give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 H_m^2=\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}
 \binom m\alpha\binom m\beta
 \prod_{v=1}^m g_{w(\alpha)_v,w(\beta)_v}
 -\left(\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}\right)^m.
\end{equation}
Indeed \(\langle W_\alpha,W_\beta\rangle\) is the displayed product.
Writing \(s_i=\sum_jg_{ij}\), the multinomial theorem gives
\(\langle F_m,G_m\rangle=(\sum_i s_i)^m/(m!)^2=\norm{G_m}_{\HS}^2\),
which proves \eqref{eq:gram}.

\paragraph{Response bound.}
For \(X_i=R_A(U_i)\) on the unit tuple ball, put
\[
 M=\sup_A\sum_i\norm{X_i}_{\op},\qquad
 K=\sup_A\sum_{i<j}\norm{[X_i,X_j]}_{\op}.
\]
If \(M=0\), all block responses vanish. For chains the positive certificate
bound below proves separation directly; otherwise assume \(M>0\).
The usual two-factor commutator representation \cite{iserles2024elementary}
gives
\[
 \norm{e^{\lambda X}e^{\lambda Y}-e^{\lambda(X+Y)}}_{\op}
 \le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\norm{[X,Y]}_{\op}
             e^{\lambda(\norm{X}_{\op}+\norm{Y}_{\op})}.
\]
For completeness, differentiate
\(e^{(\lambda-s)(X+Y)}e^{sX}e^{sY}\) and use
\[
 [e^{sX},Y]=\int_0^s e^{(s-u)X}[X,Y]e^{uX}\,du.
\]
Induction over factors, with
\([\sum_{i<j}X_i,X_j]=\sum_{i<j}[X_i,X_j]\), yields
\[
 \Del_n(\lambda)\le\frac{\lambda^2K}{2}e^{\lambda M}.
\]
Comparing power coefficients with
\(\B(\lambda)=\sum_{m\ge2}\lambda^mH_m/m!\), a sufficient condition is
\[
 K<\inf_{m\ge2}\frac{2H_m}{m(m-1)M^{m-2}}\quad(M>0).
\]
This infimum is genuinely infinite.

\paragraph{Analytic verification for chains.}
For \(U_i=[e_i,e_{i+1}]\), blocks are mutually orthogonal with norm \(\sqrt2\).
Let \(a_i=\norm{A_i}_F\) and \(c_\ell=\cos(\pi/(\ell+2))\).
The path-graph largest eigenvalue gives
\(\sum_i a_i a_{i+1}\le c_\ell\sum_i a_i^2\le c_\ell\).
B\"ottcher--Wenzel therefore gives
\[
 M\le\sqrt2c_\ell<\sqrt2,\qquad
 K\le2\sqrt2\sum_{i<j}(a_i a_{i+1})(a_j a_{j+1})
 \le\sqrt2c_\ell^2<\sqrt2.
\]
The \(K\) inequality is pointwise before taking its supremum.
Retain only nonmonotone words over the first three orthogonal blocks.
There are \(3^m-\binom{m+2}{2}\) such words, each absent from \(F_m\)
and having coefficient \(1/m!\) in \(G_m\). Hence
\[
 H_m^2\ge2^m(3^m-\binom{m+2}{2})\ge2^m3^{m-1},\qquad m\ge2.
\]
The counting inequality follows at \(m=2\) and by induction:
\(\binom{m+3}{2}/\binom{m+2}{2}=(m+3)/(m+1)<3\).
Consequently every coefficient threshold is at least
\[
 L_m=\frac{4\,3^{(m-1)/2}}{m(m-1)}.
\]
Its consecutive ratio is \(\sqrt3(m-1)/(m+1)\):
below one up to \(m=3\), and above one for \(m\ge4\).
For \(m=4+s\), the latter follows from
\(3(m-1)^2-(m+1)^2=2s^2+8s+2>0\).
Thus \(\inf_{m\ge2}L_m=L_4=\sqrt3>\sqrt2>K\).
Summing \(H_m\ge(\sqrt6)^m/\sqrt3\) gives the last bound in
\eqref{eq:chain}; the same coefficient comparison gives its middle strict
inequality. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:chain} at every positive scale.

\section{Euler minimax proof}\label{app:euler}
Put \(x_i=|A|\norm{X_i}_{1\text{-var}}\) and \(R=\sum_i x_i\).
Expanding all exact interval factors, the Euler product retains precisely
multi-indices with every interval degree at most \(N\). Factorial estimates give
\[
 \norm{\Phi^A(X)-E^A_{N,\pi}(X)}_{\op}
 \le\sum_{\max j_i>N}\prod_i\frac{x_i^{j_i}}{j_i!}
 =e^R-\prod_iP_N(x_i).
\]
The majorant increases in each \(x_i\).
For \(N\ge2\), direct coefficient comparison gives
\[
 P_{N-1}(x)^2-P_N(x)P_{N-2}(x)
 =\frac{x^{N-1}}{N!}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(N-j)\frac{x^j}{j!}\ge0.
\]
For \(N=1\), \(\log P_1=\log(1+x)\) is concave as well.
Thus \(\log P_N\) is concave, and Jensen maximizes the product at equal
\(x_i=R/m\). An equal-variation partition is independent of \(A\) and gives
the uniform bound \(\E(N,m,r)\) on the normalized path class.

For \(X_t=te_1\), take \(A_1=rQ\) with a rank-one orthogonal projection
and other matrices zero. On its range the error is
\(e^r-\prod_iP_N(r\Delta t_i)\).
Jensen shows every partition has error at least \(\E(N,m,r)\);
equal intervals attain it. This proves the quantifier order in
\eqref{eq:minimax}.

\section{Storage limit arguments}\label{app:asymp}
Write \(x=z/m\), \(a=e^{-x}\sum_{j>q}x^j/j!\), and
\(B=e^z z^{q+1}/((q+1)!m^q)\).
Poisson-tail integral bounds, or repeated integration of \(e^{-u}\), give
\[
 e^{-x}\frac{x^{q+1}}{(q+1)!}\le a\le\frac{x^{q+1}}{(q+1)!}.
\]
Since \(\E=e^z[1-(1-a)^m]\), Bernoulli's inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EB}
 \frac{e^{-x}}{1+ma}\le\frac{\E}{B}\le1.
\end{equation}
Feasibility \(\E\le\varepsilon<e^z\) and
\(1-(1-a)^m\ge1-e^{-ma}\) also imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ma}
 ma\le-\log(1-\varepsilon e^{-z}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Optimizer regime.}
For fixed \(d,k\), Witt's formula gives
\(\log\Lambda_d(kq)=\beta q-\log q+O(1)\).
Take \(q\) nearest \(\sqrt{\lambda/\beta}\) and
\[
 m=\left\lceil z\exp\left(\frac{\lambda-\log((q+1)!)}q\right)\right\rceil.
\]
This is \(B\)-feasible. Stirling and negligible relative rounding give
\(\log(C_{d,k}/z)\le2\sqrt{\beta\lambda}+O(\log\lambda)\).
Because \(m\ge1\), every optimizer has \(q=O(\sqrt\lambda)\).
In the large-\(z\) regime, \(\log z=O(\log\lambda)\) in this inference.

At fixed \(z\), bounded \(m\) and \(q=O(\sqrt\lambda)\) would, by the first
omitted term and \(1-(1-a)^m\ge a\), give
\(\log\E\ge-O(\sqrt\lambda\log\lambda)\), contradicting
\(\log\varepsilon=-\lambda+O(1)\). Thus \(m\to\infty\) and \(x\to0\).
At \(z\to\infty\), \(m=1\) gives
\(\log P_q(z)=O(\sqrt z\log z)=o(z)\), so \(\E\sim e^z\), infeasible.
If \(m\ge2\) and \(x\ge c>0\), then
\[
 \E\ge e^{z-x}\frac{x^{q+1}}{(q+1)!}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \log\E\ge z/2-O(\sqrt z\log z),
\]
also infeasible. Hence \(x\to0\), and \(m\to\infty\), in both regimes.

At fixed \(z\), \(ma\le z x^q/(q+1)!\le zx/2\to0\) once \(x<1\).
For large \(z\), \eqref{eq:ma} gives \(ma\to0\).
It follows from \eqref{eq:EB} that \(\E/B\to1\) at every optimizer, so
\(\log B\le\log\varepsilon+o(1)\).
Consequently
\[
 \log(C_{d,k}/z)\ge\beta q+\lambda/q-O(\log\lambda).
\]
The minimum of \(\beta q+\lambda/q\) is \(2\sqrt{\beta\lambda}\).
Its gap from that minimum forces \(q/\sqrt{\lambda/\beta}\to1\).
Subtracting \(\log\Lambda_d(kq)\) gives the formula for \(\log(m/z)\).

Finally, at an exact optimizer inflate \(m\) by
\(\max\{1,(B/\varepsilon)^{1/q}\}\), rounding upward.
This is \(B\)-feasible and costs a relative \(1+o(1)\) since
\(B/\varepsilon\le1+o(1)\) and \(m\to\infty\).
The exact feasible region contains the \(B\)-feasible one, proving
convergence of the cost ratio to one.

\section{Engel gauge and structured storage scope}\label{app:engel}
Repeated B\"ottcher--Wenzel gives
\[
 \norm{\ad_A^{k-1}(B)}_{\op}
 \le2^{(k-1)/2}\norm A_F^{k-1}\norm B_F.
\]
Under \(\norm A_F^2+\norm B_F^2\le1\), the maximum occurs at squared
norms \((k-1)/k\) and \(1/k\), giving \(g_k\).
A diagonal block \(A=\sqrt{(k-1)/(2k)}\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\)
and \(B=E_{12}/\sqrt k\) attain operator norm \(g_k\).
Binomial expansion of \(E_k\) gives HS norm \(h_k\).
Its ratio \(g_k/h_k\) decays exponentially in \(k\); this is a sharp
corollary of a classical commutator inequality.

For the pure weakly geometric path \(\exp_\otimes(taE_k)\), use \(p\ge k\).
At Euler depth \(kq\), coefficientwise nonnegative error terms give
\[
 \E(q,m,a s_k)\le\delta_k(q,m,a)\le\E(q,m,a g_k)<\E(q,m,a h_k).
\]
For the lower witness, set \(\alpha=\sqrt{(k-1)/k}\), \(\eta=1/\sqrt k\):
\[
 A_1=\frac\alpha{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A_2=\frac\eta{\sqrt2}
                 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\(-1)^{k-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their unit tuple evaluates \(E_k\) to a symmetric matrix with off-diagonal
entries \(s_k\), hence positive eigenvalue \(s_k\).
Jensen gives the same witness lower bound for adaptive partitions.
For the HS equality, each tensor-degree-\(kj\) error is
\(c_j a^j E_k^{\otimes j}\) with \(c_j\ge0\).
Its norm is \(c_j(a h_k)^j\), whose sum equals \(\E(q,m,a h_k)\).

For \(\varepsilon<e^{a s_k}-1\), depths below \(k\) are infeasible;
intermediate depths have the same polynomial as the preceding multiple of
\(k\) and larger storage. This gives the stated storage sandwich.
The amplitude corollary follows from \eqref{eq:asymp}.
At fixed \(a\), all bracket endpoints instead have common leading
logarithmic rate \(2\sqrt{k\log d\log(1/\varepsilon)}\).

\section{Rational certificates and reproducibility}\label{app:cert}
For rational \(x\ge0\) and integer \(J\) with \(x<J+2\),
\[
 P_J(x)\le e^x\le
 P_J(x)+\frac{x^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}\frac1{1-x/(J+2)}.
\]
This follows by a geometric upper bound on all successive omitted-term
ratios. All displayed example enclosures use \(J=24\).
Integer squares verify
\[
 \sqrt2<707107/500000,\quad
 \sqrt3>34641/20000,\quad \sqrt6<244949/100000.
\]
For the corner, \(\rho h=\sqrt6/27\).
Positive coefficients permit replacing \(\sqrt2\) parameters by their upper
bounds in \(Q_R\); apply the exponential enclosure, and use the lower
\(\sqrt3\) bound in \(ht^3\). Exact fractions then give \eqref{eq:corner}.

For the storage example, bound \(e=e^1\) by the same rational enclosure.
The first five cumulative dimensions are \(2,3,5,8,14\).
Enumerating every \((N,m)\) of cost at most 8 for \(\E\), and at most 14
for \(B\), gives the two unique optima in the table. Higher depths already
cost more with \(m=1\). Each feasibility inequality is strictly decided by
the rational bounds.
